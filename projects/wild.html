Tropical Evergreen

Western Ghats, Northeast India, Andamans

Found in areas receiving over 200cm of annual rainfall. These forests are dense, multi-layered, and retain their green canopy year-round, supporting immense biodiversity.

Tropical Deciduous

Central & South India, Himalayan Foothills

Also known as "Monsoon Forests," these are the most widespread in India. Trees shed their leaves during the dry spring to conserve water before the monsoon arrives.

Tropical Thorn

Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana

Found in arid, semi-desert regions with less than 70cm of rainfall. Characterized by scattered, deep-rooted thorny trees (like Babool) and extensive scrub vegetation.

Montane (Mountain)

Himalayas, High-altitude Western Ghats

These forests change drastically with altitude, transitioning from subtropical pines at the base, to temperate oaks, and finally to alpine mosses and junipers near the snowline.

Mangrove (Tidal)

Sundarbans, Mahanadi & Godavari Deltas

Found in coastal estuarine regions affected by tides. The trees have specialized aerial "breathing roots" that allow them to survive in harsh, muddy, brackish saltwater.
